How to Help Your Distributors Sell More of Your Product

Distributors carry many brands. The ones that get attention are the easiest to understand, explain and sell. Here is how to become one of them.

Rooftop Sales Team · 11 Nov 2026 · Updated 13 Nov 2026 · 2 min read

Home / Journal / How to Help Your Distributors Sell More of Your Product

Key takeaways

  • Distributors focus on products that are easy to sell.
  • Train the partner's sales team, not just the owner.
  • Give simple tools: a clear pitch, comparison points and answers to common objections.
  • Agree shared goals and review them regularly.
  • Recognise and reward partner success.

To grow distribution channel sales, make your product the easiest one in the distributor's range to explain and sell. Train their sales team, give them simple tools, agree shared goals and stay in regular contact. Distributors carry many brands, so the support you give often decides how much attention your product gets.

Why do distributors favour some brands over others?

A distributor's sales team is busy and usually represents many products. When they speak to a customer, they lean on what they understand best and what they feel confident recommending. If your product is hard to explain, it gets left out of the conversation, even if it is a strong product.

Five ways to grow distribution channel sales

1. Train the people who sell

Owners and managers may know your product well, but the sellers on the front line are the ones talking to customers. Short, practical sessions work better than long presentations. Focus on who the product is for, the problem it solves and how to answer common questions.

2. Make the product easy to explain

Give partners a simple story they can repeat in a minute: the customer, the problem and the result. Support it with a one-page guide and clear comparison points against the alternatives customers usually consider.

3. Prepare them for objections

List the questions and concerns customers raise most often, with short, honest answers. When sellers feel ready for tough questions, they are more willing to bring your product up.

4. Agree shared goals

Set targets together rather than handing them down. Agree which customers or segments to focus on, what support you will provide and how you will both measure progress. Review these goals at a regular, agreed interval.

5. Keep a steady rhythm of support

A partner who hears from you only when targets are missed will not feel supported. Regular check-ins, joint customer visits and quick answers to questions all build trust and keep your product top of mind.

What partners find most useful

Measure what matters

Look beyond total volume. Track which partners and which sellers are actively selling your product, which customer types are buying and where deals stall. These signals show you where more training or support will make the biggest difference.

Share what you learn with your partners as well. When a distributor can see which customer types respond best and which messages work, they can plan their own sales effort with more confidence. Frequently asked questions

How can I get distributors to focus on my product?

Make it easy to sell. Train their sellers, give them a simple pitch and objection answers, and stay in regular contact so your product stays top of mind.

What should distributor sales training include?

Who the product is for, the problem it solves, how it compares with alternatives, and short answers to the questions customers ask most often.

How do I measure distributor performance?

Look at more than total sales: how many sellers are active with your product, which customers buy and where deals stall.
